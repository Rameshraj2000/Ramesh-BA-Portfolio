CASE STUDY 02 · REQUIREMENTS ENGINEERING

Business Process & Requirements Analysis

An industry-style case study demonstrating how a business need can be explored, translated into requirements and prepared for validation.

Stakeholder analysisBRD / FRDUser storiesRTMUAT

Purpose

This case study was created to demonstrate a structured BA workflow from project initiation through requirements and validation. The emphasis is on documentation and traceability: making needs understandable to stakeholders and testable by delivery teams.

Project type: Portfolio case study. This page describes the activities documented in the case study; it does not claim a production implementation or measured business impact.

Work completed

  1. Project initiation: clarify the problem context, objectives, scope and assumptions.
  2. Stakeholder analysis: identify relevant stakeholder groups, their interests and information needs.
  3. AS-IS analysis: describe the current process and identify pain points or gaps to investigate.
  4. Requirement analysis: organise business needs and functional expectations into structured documentation.
  5. TO-BE design: describe the proposed future process and how it addresses the identified gaps.
  6. User stories and acceptance criteria: express requirements from a user perspective and define testable expected outcomes.
  7. Wireframes and RTM: visualise proposed interactions and maintain traceability between requirements and validation.

Key BA artefacts

  • Stakeholder analysis and process context
  • AS-IS / TO-BE process comparison
  • Business and functional requirements (BRD / FRD structure)
  • User stories and acceptance criteria
  • Wireframes
  • Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM)
  • Validation and UAT-oriented test cases

How I would use this in delivery

In a live project, I would validate assumptions with stakeholders, confirm requirement priority and scope, review acceptance criteria with developers and testers, maintain traceability as requirements change, and use UAT evidence to determine whether the agreed business need has been met.

Where stakeholders disagree or the process is ambiguous, I would document the decision needed, the options and impacts, and the owner responsible for resolution rather than silently choosing a solution.

What this case study demonstrates

The case study shows a requirements-led approach: understand the current state, define the desired state, translate needs into clear and testable requirements, and preserve traceability through validation. It is intended as a discussion piece for BA interviews, especially questions about elicitation, process analysis, requirement quality and UAT.
