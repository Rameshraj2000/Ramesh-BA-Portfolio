CASE STUDY 01 · SALES ANALYTICS

Sales Performance & Profitability Analysis

Investigating why strong sales did not consistently translate into strong profit using data profiling, hypothesis-driven SQL analysis and Power BI decision support.

Business analysisMySQLPower BIRoot-cause analysis

The business question

Why are sales relatively strong while profitability remains comparatively weak? I framed the work as an investigation rather than starting with charts, translating the broad concern into questions about region, category, sub-category, product losses and discount levels.

Project type: Independent portfolio case study using the Superstore Sales dataset. Findings are analytical signals from the available data, not confirmed operational outcomes.

Approach

  1. Define the problem and questions. Establish sales, profit and margin baselines and identify the dimensions that could explain the gap.
  2. Profile and validate data. Review field definitions, data types, missing values, duplicates and record counts before interpreting results.
  3. Test hypotheses with SQL. Compare profitability by region, category, sub-category, product and discount band.
  4. Drill into the strongest signal. Follow the Furniture → Tables → discount → regional pathway to understand where losses were concentrated.
  5. Translate findings into action. Define management questions, recommendations and KPIs, then organise them into Power BI views.

Selected findings

12.45%Overall SQL profit margin
2.32%Furniture SQL profit margin
−8.56%Tables SQL profit margin
  • Central had the lowest overall regional SQL margin at 8.06%, despite approximately $497.8K in sales.
  • Furniture generated approximately $733.0K in sales but only a 2.32% SQL margin.
  • Tables generated approximately $206.97K in sales and approximately $17.73K in negative profit.
  • Profitability weakened as discount levels increased; the analysis found negative margins at 30% discount and above.
  • Within Tables, East showed the most severe regional loss exposure in the analysis, while West had the highest Tables sales but weak profitability.

Data quality limitation: the source dataset contained 9,994 records while the MySQL population contained 9,694, a 300-record difference. The Power BI executive view and SQL baseline therefore differ slightly. The discount pattern is an association, not proof of causation.

Recommendations for management review

  • Review high-discount Tables transactions and the reasons behind their discount levels.
  • Consider discount guardrails or approval thresholds for transactions that could become loss-making.
  • Review regional discounting and product mix, distinguishing overall regional performance from the specific Tables loss pathway.
  • Monitor sales, profit, margin, discount and loss-making products together rather than relying on revenue alone.

These are proposed actions based on the portfolio analysis; they have not been implemented or measured in a live business.

Deliverables & learning

Deliverables included a data dictionary and profiling, SQL analysis, a hypothesis and findings narrative, stakeholder-oriented recommendations, and a three-page Power BI dashboard covering executive overview, profitability deep dive, and product/loss analysis.

The key lesson was to keep data limitations visible and distinguish correlation from causation while tracing a broad business problem into a specific, actionable area.
