CASE STUDY 03 · PRODUCT & INVENTORY ANALYTICS

Maven Toys — Product & Inventory Performance

An end-to-end portfolio case study combining business questions, SQL analysis, Power BI / DAX, requirements, recommendations and UAT.

SQLPower BIDAXRequirementsUAT

The business problem

The case study explored how management could get a consolidated view of product, category, store and product–store inventory performance. The analysis focused on performance and inventory-position signals that could guide further investigation, not on asserting unobserved operational outcomes.

Project type: Independent portfolio case study using the Maven Toys dataset. It is not employment at Maven Toys and does not claim the recommendations were implemented by a real business.

Dataset and analysis grain

1.09MUnits analysed
$14.44MRevenue analysed
$4.01MGross profit analysed

The dataset included 829,262 sales rows, 35 products, 50 stores and 1,593 store–product inventory records, with calendar coverage from 1 January 2022 to 30 September 2023.

Sales were transaction-level while inventory was a current Store × Product snapshot. A key modelling lesson was to aggregate fact tables at the appropriate grain before joining them; directly joining raw sales transactions to inventory could multiply inventory rows and inflate totals.

Approach

  1. Define business questions and KPIs around product/category performance, profitability, store performance and inventory risk.
  2. Profile and validate source data, review field definitions and check key relationships across sales, products, stores, inventory and calendar.
  3. Analyse with SQL at the correct grain to avoid inflated aggregates and identify product, store and product–store signals.
  4. Build a four-page Power BI dashboard using DAX measures for revenue, gross profit, gross margin, inventory value, average daily demand and Days of Supply.
  5. Document requirements and validation through user stories, acceptance criteria, an RTM, wireframes and UAT cases.

Findings and interpretation

  • Product and category results differed across units, revenue and gross profit, so no single measure was treated as a complete performance picture.
  • Store rankings highlighted differences in revenue and profitability efficiency that could be explored by stakeholders.
  • Product × Store positions with low or zero stock, or unusually high Days of Supply, were treated as investigation signals relative to historical demand.
  • These signals do not by themselves prove lost sales, confirmed excess stock or a specific operational cause.

UAT and validation

12 / 12Planned UAT cases passed
0Failed cases
0Blocked or open defects

UAT checked the dashboard and expected behaviour against the documented scenarios. The recorded result was 12 passed tests, with zero failed, blocked or open defects in the planned test set.

Recommendations and learning

The dashboard was designed to help stakeholders prioritise follow-up on product and store performance, investigate inventory coverage exceptions, and consider both demand and inventory position before making decisions. Recommendations should be validated against current operational context before action.

The project reinforced the importance of data grain, explicit KPI definitions, requirements traceability and communicating the difference between an analytical signal and a proven business outcome.
